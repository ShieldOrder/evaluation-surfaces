# Legitimacy Laundering

Status: failure mode definition  
Scope: misuse of process-derived legitimacy  
Posture: descriptive, non-prescriptive

## Definition

Legitimacy laundering occurs when the *existence* of a governance process or
signal is used to justify an outcome, independent of whether that process or
signal was fit for the decision surface involved.

The decision is framed as legitimate because “a process occurred,” not because
the decision met its correctness, accountability, or fiduciary requirements.

## Core pattern

1. A decision is made or desired
2. A legitimacy signal is invoked after the fact
3. The signal substitutes for evaluation rather than supporting it

The signal functions as a shield, not as evidence.

## Distinction from signal collapse

- **Signal collapse** is a category error
- **Legitimacy laundering** is a justification error

Signal collapse misapplies a signal.  
Legitimacy laundering retroactively uses a signal to sanctify an outcome.

They often co-occur, but are not identical.

## Common laundering techniques

### Process invocation

Citing the presence of a vote, poll, discussion, or committee as sufficient
legitimacy without examining whether the process measured what mattered.

Examples:
- “There was a vote”
- “The committee approved it”
- “The community discussed it”

### Procedural finality claims

Treating procedural completion as decision correctness.

Once the process ends, further evaluation is framed as illegitimate or
disruptive.

### Retroactive framing

Re-describing the decision surface to match the signal used.

For example:
- A correctness decision reframed as preference
- A fiduciary decision reframed as sentiment
- An accountability decision reframed as consensus

### Accountability deflection

Using the process to diffuse responsibility:
- “The process decided”
- “The community wanted this”
- “We followed procedure”

No actor remains accountable for outcomes.

## Why legitimacy laundering is attractive

Legitimacy laundering:
- reduces personal responsibility
- converts judgment into compliance
- short-circuits difficult evaluation
- resolves disputes socially rather than analytically

It is especially prevalent in contested or high-risk decisions.

## Detection heuristic

Legitimacy laundering is likely when:

- legitimacy is asserted without reference to decision fitness
- criticism is dismissed as “anti-process”
- outcomes are defended by citing participation alone
- no clear owner exists for failure consequences

## Relationship to this repository

This repository separates:
- decision surfaces (what is being decided)
- legitimacy signals (what is being measured)
- failure modes (how misuse occurs)

Legitimacy laundering is one such misuse.

Explicit separation makes laundering visible and contestable.
