# Failure Modes

Status: analytical taxonomy  
Scope: governance misuse patterns  
Posture: descriptive, non-prescriptive

## Purpose

This directory documents recurring failure modes that arise when legitimacy
signals are misapplied to decision surfaces.

These are not policy critiques. They are diagnostic patterns that explain
*how* governance processes fail, even when actors believe they are acting
procedurally correctly.

## What belongs here

A failure mode belongs in this directory if:

- a governance process exists
- a legitimacy signal is present
- outcomes are justified procedurally
- decision fitness, correctness, or accountability is bypassed

The focus is misuse, not intent.

## Relationship to other directories

- **surfaces/** define *what kind of decision* is being made
- **signals/** define *what is being measured*
- **failure-modes/** define *how signals are misused*

Failure modes emerge at the boundary between signals and surfaces.

## Included failure modes

### Legitimacy Laundering

Using the existence of a process or signal to justify an outcome, independent
of whether the signal was fit for the decision surface.

The process becomes a shield rather than evidence.

Additional failure modes may be added as they are observed or formalized.

## Non-goals

This directory does not:
- propose governance reforms
- recommend signal hierarchies
- assign blame or intent
- advocate for or against any mechanism

## Use

These documents are intended to be referenced *situationally*:
- during disputes
- when outcomes are defended procedurally
- when accountability is unclear
- when signals are treated as binding without fitness analysis

They are infrastructure for clearer reasoning, not arguments.
