# Resource Allocation Decision Surface

Status: surface definition  
Scope: allocation of shared financial or operational resources  
Posture: descriptive, non-prescriptive

---

## Definition

A **resource allocation decision** determines how limited shared resources
are distributed among competing uses.

These decisions are not about technical correctness, but about prioritization
under constraint.

---

## Primary characteristics

Resource allocation decisions are characterized by:

- scarcity and trade-offs
- opportunity cost
- comparative evaluation rather than absolute truth
- reversibility over time (relative to protocol correctness)

Examples include:
- grant approvals or rejections
- budget sizing
- funding prioritization
- staffing or contractor selection
- sequencing of initiatives

---

## Evaluation requirements

Sound evaluation on this surface requires:

- clear criteria for comparison
- transparency of assumptions
- proportionality between cost and expected benefit
- consideration of downside and failure risk
- accountability for outcomes

No single signal is sufficient on its own.

---

## Legitimate signals (high fitness)

Signals that provide meaningful information on this surface include:

- committee evaluation and deliberation
- track record and execution history
- milestone clarity and verifiability
- budget discipline and burn analysis
- expert input relevant to delivery risk

These signals support comparative judgment.

---

## Supporting but limited signals (medium fitness)

Signals that may inform but should not bind outcomes:

- community preference or sentiment
- economic exposure alignment
- popularity or visibility
- narrative momentum

These signals do not measure execution quality or fiduciary soundness.

---

## Weak or invalid signals (low fitness)

Signals that are weak or misleading on this surface include:

- technical correctness proofs alone
- binary voting without evaluation criteria
- urgency framing without trade-off analysis
- post hoc justification after allocation

These signals obscure opportunity cost.

---

## Failure modes

Common failure modes when this surface is mis-evaluated:

- **Preference capture**  
  Allocation follows popularity rather than merit.

- **Accountability dilution**  
  Outcomes lack responsible owners.

- **Budgetary drift**  
  Scope expands without proportional benefit.

- **Process substitution**  
  Procedure replaces judgment.

---

## Boundary conditions

Resource allocation decisions may be informed by preference signals,
but require explicit evaluation logic and accountable decision-makers.

This surface governs stewardship, not consensus.
