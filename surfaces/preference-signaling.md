# Preference and Sentiment Signaling Decision Surface

Status: surface definition  
Scope: expression of stakeholder preferences and directional input  
Posture: descriptive, non-prescriptive

---

## Definition

A **preference and sentiment signaling decision** captures stakeholder views
about direction, prioritization, or desirability.

This surface measures *what stakeholders want*, not *what is correct*,
*what is safe*, or *what has been delivered*.

---

## Primary characteristics

Preference signaling decisions are characterized by:

- expression rather than enforcement
- aggregation of views
- absence of binding authority
- sensitivity to framing and participation
- weak accountability guarantees

Examples include:
- prioritization polls
- advisory votes
- surveys and sentiment gathering
- informal signaling mechanisms

---

## Evaluation requirements

Sound evaluation on this surface requires:

- clarity about non-binding status
- transparency of participation criteria
- resistance to misinterpretation as authority
- explicit separation from execution or correctness decisions

Preference signals inform. They do not decide.

---

## Legitimate signals (high fitness)

Signals that are well-suited to this surface include:

- stakeholder surveys
- advisory voting mechanisms
- sentiment polling
- structured feedback processes

These signals measure preference intensity and distribution.

---

## Supporting but limited signals (medium fitness)

Signals that may contextualize preferences but should not be over-weighted:

- public discourse
- social media engagement
- narrative advocacy
- endorsements

These signals amplify views but distort representativeness.

---

## Weak or invalid signals (low fitness)

Signals that are weak or misleading on this surface include:

- urgency framing
- authority appeals
- technical arguments
- execution claims

These signals do not measure preference.

---

## Failure modes

Common failure modes when this surface is misapplied:

- **Authority substitution**  
  Preference signals are treated as binding decisions.

- **Scope bleed**  
  Sentiment is used to justify correctness or execution outcomes.

- **Legitimacy inflation**  
  Signal existence is mistaken for mandate.

- **Participation distortion**  
  Outcomes reflect access or coordination rather than preference.

---

## Boundary conditions

Preference signaling must remain advisory.

Once treated as authoritative outside its scope, it ceases to be a signal
and becomes a governance error.

This surface expresses desire, not obligation.
