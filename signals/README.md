# Legitimacy Signals

Status: analytical taxonomy  
Scope: classification of governance legitimacy signals  
Posture: descriptive, non-prescriptive

## Purpose

This directory defines **legitimacy signals** used in governance contexts.

A legitimacy signal is an input used to justify, support, or contextualize a decision.
Signals do not determine *what kind of decision* is being made. They are evaluated
relative to a decision surface.

This separation is intentional.

## Signal vs surface

- A **decision surface** answers: what type of decision is this?
- A **legitimacy signal** answers: what evidence or input is being used to justify it?

Errors occur when a signal is treated as universally authoritative,
rather than surface-dependent.

## Design principles

All signal definitions in this directory follow these constraints:

- no advocacy
- no prescriptions
- no normative ranking
- explicit limits
- surface-relative fitness only

Signals are analyzed for what they measure well
and what they cannot measure.

## Included signal categories

This directory may include, but is not limited to:

- economic exposure based signals
- expert judgment
- committee evaluation
- open discussion and discourse
- process compliance signals

Inclusion does not imply endorsement.

## Interpretation rule

A signal is only legitimate when applied to a decision surface it can
credibly evaluate.

Using a signal outside its fitness domain creates governance failure modes
documented elsewhere in this repository.
