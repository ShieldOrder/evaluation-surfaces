# Expert Review

Status: signal definition  
Scope: correctness, safety, feasibility assessment  
Posture: descriptive, non-prescriptive

## Definition

Expert review is a legitimacy signal based on evaluation by individuals or groups
with demonstrated technical competence relevant to the decision surface.

The signal derives from expertise, not authority or representation.

## What this signal measures well

Expert review is well suited to measure:

- technical correctness
- security and safety properties
- feasibility constraints
- implementation risks
- design soundness
- interaction effects and edge cases

This signal is strongest when decisions require correctness rather than preference.

## What this signal does not measure

Expert review does not measure:

- stakeholder preference
- economic exposure alignment
- social legitimacy
- prioritization among competing values
- willingness to fund or tolerate outcomes

Expert consensus does not imply desirability.

## Surface fitness

Best-fit decision surfaces:

- protocol correctness
- execution and accountability (technical verification aspects)

Weak or invalid fit:

- preference and sentiment signaling
- value tradeoff decisions absent correctness constraints

Using expert review to infer preference is a category error.

## Failure modes

Common failure modes include:

- credential inflation (treating reputation as authority)
- opacity of reasoning
- unbounded scope creep
- conflating feasibility with endorsement
- substituting review for accountability

Expert review evaluates claims, not outcomes.

## Relationship to other signals

Expert review often operates alongside:

- committee evaluation
- economic exposure voting
- open discussion

Disagreement between experts and other signals is expected and unresolved here.

## Interpretation constraint

Expert review provides correctness assessment, not legitimacy by itself.

Correctness does not bind collective choice unless explicitly elevated to authority.
