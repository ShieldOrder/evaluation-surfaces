# Committee Evaluation

Status: signal definition  
Scope: comparative judgment and fiduciary oversight  
Posture: descriptive, non-prescriptive

## Definition

Committee evaluation is a legitimacy signal produced by a bounded group
explicitly tasked with assessing proposals or decisions against defined criteria.

The signal derives from delegated mandate and process, not technical authority
or economic exposure.

## What this signal measures well

Committee evaluation is well suited to measure:

- comparative quality across proposals
- alignment with stated objectives
- budget discipline and prioritization
- milestone structure and deliverability
- consistency with established policy or scope

This signal is strongest when decisions require structured tradeoffs.

## What this signal does not measure

Committee evaluation does not measure:

- protocol correctness or security guarantees
- cryptographic soundness
- broad stakeholder preference
- economic exposure alignment
- long-term systemic risk beyond mandate scope

Committee approval does not imply technical safety.

## Surface fitness

Best-fit decision surfaces:

- resource allocation
- execution and accountability

Weak or invalid fit:

- protocol correctness
- preference and sentiment signaling at ecosystem scale

Using committee judgment to assert correctness is a category error.

## Failure modes

Common failure modes include:

- mandate drift
- criteria ambiguity
- informal authority inflation
- capture by narrative framing
- deferral to external signals without scrutiny

Committees evaluate within scope; scope violations undermine legitimacy.

## Relationship to other signals

Committee evaluation often interacts with:

- expert review (for correctness inputs)
- preference signaling (for directional context)
- open discussion (for qualitative feedback)

Signal conflict is expected and unresolved here.

## Interpretation constraint

Committee evaluation binds outcomes only within its delegated authority.

Beyond that boundary, it is advisory.
